Documented work

Co-authored Cognitive-Chair, a brain-sensing wheelchair research project for paraplegic and quadriplegic people. The work explores brain–computer interfaces, EEG, and EOG for assistive mobility. Published in IEEE Xplore in 2023.

The problem

People with severe mobility limitations may need alternatives to conventional wheelchair controls. Cognitive-Chair explores brain-sensing and human–machine interfaces for assistive mobility.

Approach & contribution

  • Investigate EEG and EOG signals as inputs for wheelchair control.
  • Process recorded signals into information suitable for command interpretation.
  • Explore classification of processed signals into machine-readable commands, as described in the historical project page.
  • Connect software and hardware through a brain–computer interface.

System workflow

High-level documented workflow.

  1. Neural signals — EEG and EOG provide the brain–computer interface signals for Cognitive-Chair.
  2. Process — Process the acquired signals before interpreting potential commands.
  3. Interpret — Interpret processed signals as part of the assistive-mobility workflow.
  4. Movement — Connect interpreted commands with the wheelchair movement concept described in the publication.
